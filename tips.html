Packaging Tips

Practical, time-tested guidance on choosing, using and storing corrugated packaging, so your products arrive safely and your packaging budget goes further.

Tip 01

Choose the right ply

Match board strength to product weight and journey.

  • 3 ply suits lighter products, retail packs and short, gentle transit.
  • 5 ply is the all-rounder for medium-weight goods, longer routes and moderate stacking.
  • 7 ply is for heavy, industrial or export loads that must survive tall stacks and rough handling.
  • When unsure, share the product weight and how high boxes will be stacked; that decides the ply.
Tip 02

Pick the right flute

Flute height changes cushioning, strength and print quality.

  • Larger (jumbo) flutes give more cushioning and vertical stacking strength.
  • B-flute offers good crush and puncture resistance with a flatter surface for printing.
  • Narrow flutes are thin and smooth: ideal for neat retail cartons and sharp graphics.
  • Combining flutes in 5 and 7 ply board blends strength with a good print face.
Tip 03

Right-size every box

A snug fit protects better and costs less.

  • Oversized boxes let products move, crush under load and need more filler.
  • Excess empty space also raises freight cost, since couriers charge on volume too.
  • Always give internal dimensions in Length × Width × Height order.
  • Allow a little clearance for inserts or dividers, but no more.
Tip 04

Cushion fragile items

Inner cartons and cushioning inserts stop shocks before they reach the product.

  • Pack each unit in its own inner carton, then group them in a master carton.
  • Use cushioning inserts or corrugated partitions sized to the product so nothing moves inside.
  • Keep fragile items away from the outer walls, where knocks land first.
  • Shake-test a packed sample: if you hear movement, add support.
Tip 05

Separate bottles & small parts

Partitions and dividers prevent items knocking against each other.

  • Corrugated partitions give each bottle or jar its own cell.
  • Die-cut inserts can hold small components in their own slots.
  • Separated items are easier to count, inspect and unpack.
  • Design the cell or slot size around the actual product, not a generic size.
Tip 06

Go heavy-duty for export

Long, multi-handled journeys need extra strength.

  • Honeycomb cartons give high compression strength at a low weight.
  • Pallet boxes and layer pads keep bulk loads stable on pallets.
  • Plan for humid sea routes and long storage times when choosing board grade.
  • Ask for higher ply or honeycomb when goods will be stacked for weeks.
Tip 07

Keep corrugated dry

Moisture is the biggest enemy of box strength.

  • Damp board loses much of its stacking strength, so store boxes in a dry, ventilated area.
  • Keep stock off the floor on pallets and a little away from walls.
  • Avoid storing near water sources, open doors or direct sunlight.
  • Use older stock first (first in, first out) and keep bundles wrapped until use.
Tip 08

Seal it properly

The right taping keeps boxes closed and square.

  • Use the H-taping method: seal the centre seam and both edge seams.
  • Choose good-quality tape wide enough for the box size and weight.
  • Press tape firmly along its full length so it bonds to the board.
  • Heavier boxes benefit from taping on both the top and bottom flaps.
Tip 09

Stack & palletise smartly

How boxes are stacked matters as much as the box itself.

  • Column stacking (corners aligned) keeps the most strength in the walls.
  • Never let cartons overhang the pallet edge; unsupported edges weaken fast.
  • Place the heaviest boxes at the bottom and use layer pads between tiers.
  • Stretch-wrap pallets to stop loads shifting in transit.
Tip 10

Print for brand & handling

Your master carton is a moving billboard and an instruction sheet.

  • Add your logo and product details so cartons are identified at a glance.
  • Include handling symbols such as This Way Up, Fragile and Keep Dry.
  • Bold, high-contrast artwork prints best on corrugated board.
  • Mark stacking limits and box contents to speed up warehouse handling.
Tip 11

Standardise to save

Fewer box sizes mean simpler stock and better pricing.

  • Review your range: several products can often share one well-designed carton.
  • Standard sizes reduce storage space and ordering effort.
  • Plan regular volumes with your supplier for smoother production and delivery.
  • Revisit packaging when products or shipping routes change.
Tip 12

Choose sustainable packaging

Corrugated board is paper-based and widely recyclable.

  • Corrugated boxes are made from paper fibre and can be recycled after use.
  • Right-sizing cuts the material you buy and the waste your customers handle.
  • Use paper-based fillers, partitions and dividers to protect products while keeping the pack recyclable.
  • Encourage reuse of sturdy master cartons within your own supply chain.

These are general packaging guidelines. Every product is different. Talk to our team for a recommendation built around your product, weight and shipping route.
